# Pera Property Client Communication Guide

**Purpose:** An operational guide for maintaining accurate, consistent and natural Pera Property client communication across email and WhatsApp. This repository version contains reusable business guidance only; identifiable client records must be maintained in a private system.

**Last updated:** 2 October 2026

## How to use this file

When Mustafa shares a client conversation:

1. Identify the client and channel.
2. Extract facts without embellishment.
3. Separate client-specific information from reusable Pera knowledge.
4. Record promises, quoted fees, deadlines and follow-up actions exactly.
5. Flag contradictions rather than silently replacing earlier information.
6. Draft replies using the latest confirmed information in this file.
7. Date material updates so changes in circumstances remain traceable.
8. Where useful, select a relevant article from the Pera content link index and embed it naturally in the reply.

Do not treat a client's stated belief as a verified fact. Do not turn a one-off commercial concession into a general Pera policy. Do not infer nationality, budget, legal status, financing ability or investment objective unless the client has stated it.

## Communication principles

- Write as a knowledgeable Istanbul property adviser, not as a generic salesperson.
- Be candid about weak investments, oversupply, inflated developer pricing and exit risk.
- Explain the reasoning behind recommendations.
- Never imply that citizenship eligibility proves investment quality.
- Distinguish asking prices, developer price lists, comparable resales and achieved transaction prices.
- Avoid promises about capital appreciation, rental income, citizenship approval, valuations or completion dates.
- State uncertainty plainly and identify what must be verified.
- Keep WhatsApp replies concise and conversational. Emails may be fuller and more structured.
- Preserve Mustafa's natural voice. Minor human imperfections may be intentional; do not automatically make personal emails sound polished by AI.
- Avoid excessive headings, bullet points and promotional language in client-facing drafts.
- Do not repeat information the client has already acknowledged unless it is necessary for the decision.

### First-enquiry and WhatsApp responses

- Open by identifying the adviser and Pera Property, then refer to the exact property or subject of the enquiry. Where useful, include the current Pera property link so both parties are discussing the same listing.
- Answer the enquiry before trying to broaden it. A first response should normally give the important known facts, explain what can be sent or arranged next, and end with one useful question rather than a long questionnaire.
- WhatsApp messages should use short paragraphs and plain English. They may be less formal than email, but pricing, availability, tenancy, commission and legal statements must remain precise.
- If a telephone call was attempted, mention it once and ask for a convenient time. Do not make the message depend on the recipient accepting a call; provide enough useful information to encourage a reply.
- Where the enquiry comes from an adviser or intermediary acting for a private client, acknowledge that relationship and state that a commission-sharing arrangement can be discussed. Do not promise a percentage until the commission available on that property and the introducer terms have been confirmed.
- Attach or offer the specific material requested, such as the floor plan, brochure, current photographs or video. Do not claim that an attachment has been included unless it is actually being sent.
- Ask questions that change the recommendation: own use or investment, approximate timing, funding position, citizenship requirement, preferred size, and whether the client can visit or needs a video viewing. Do not ask all of these mechanically when only one or two are needed.
- For a Pera-managed resale, say that Pera has managed the apartment only where this is confirmed. This is valuable because it establishes direct knowledge of the property and tenancy history.
- Distinguish clearly between the advertised price, the owner's likely negotiating position and any suggested offer. Do not silently replace one with another.
- Describe a seller as motivated or as no longer having a personal need for the property. Avoid disclosing age, health or other unnecessary personal information.
- For occupied property, state the current rent and estimated market rent as approximate figures when appropriate. Treat a tenant's expected departure, rent increase or vacant-possession date as unconfirmed until documented; never present it as guaranteed.
- Development facilities and location claims should be taken from the current listing or verified project information. Keep the location paragraph practical: district character, nearby recognised destinations and access to the main business or lifestyle areas.

## Source and confidence rules

Use these labels when recording knowledge internally:

- **Confirmed by Mustafa:** Treat as authoritative Pera knowledge unless later corrected.
- **Confirmed by client:** Reliable for that client's circumstances, but not independently verified.
- **Documented:** Supported by a contract, listing, brochure, official record or other supplied document.
- **Externally verified:** Checked against a current authoritative source.
- **Working assumption:** May be used for analysis but must not be presented as confirmed.
- **Superseded:** Retained for history but must not be used in new replies.

For time-sensitive legal, tax, immigration, financing, pricing or regulatory matters, verify the current position before giving definitive guidance.

## Pera's established advisory positions

### Investment quality

- A property should make sense to the Turkish rental and resale market, not only to international buyers.
- Domestic resale depth, building quality, title status, management, transport and realistic pricing are central considerations.
- New-build status and developer branding do not automatically make a property a good investment.
- International sales commissions and foreign-buyer premiums can make some projects difficult to resell.
- High service charges, large competing inventory and investor-oriented layouts can weaken actual returns.
- Infrastructure should be valued according to what is operating or credibly delivered, not only what is promised.

### Turkish citizenship by investment

- Citizenship eligibility is a legal feature, not proof of fair value or resale liquidity.
- Many qualifying opportunities are new builds because valuation and eligibility can be more straightforward than with ordinary resale property.
- Resale properties that genuinely satisfy citizenship requirements may exist, but they are relatively uncommon and require careful legal and valuation checks.
- Never guarantee eligibility, valuation acceptance or citizenship approval.

### Client recommendations

- Start with the client's objective: use, income, capital preservation, citizenship, relocation or a combination.
- Confirm budget, preferred currency, intended holding period and tolerance for vacancy or resale delay.
- Explain trade-offs rather than claiming that one district or property is universally best.
- Where possible, support recommendations with genuine comparable resales and realistic rental evidence.

## Istanbul market knowledge relevant to conversations

### Central and business districts

- Şişli, Nişantaşı, Levent, Maslak and Bomonti benefit from employment, transport, hospitals, universities and established demand.
- Beyoğlu is a building-by-building heritage market. Title structure, building condition, access, noise and short-let rules require particular attention.
- Kağıthane and Seyrantepe benefit from delivered road and rail infrastructure, but supply and micro-location vary materially.
- **Maslak naming:** “Maslak” is often used loosely for genuine Maslak and the Vadi İstanbul/Seyrantepe area. State the real location. Genuine Maslak is expensive and can offer poor value where the same budget buys only a one-bedroom apartment; compare it directly with more central Şişli stock. **Source: Confirmed by Mustafa, 2026-09-08.**

### Regeneration and value-led districts

- Gaziosmanpaşa and central Eyüpsultan are domestic, value-led markets supported by dense local demand and transport.
- Central Eyüp/Alibeyköy should be distinguished from Göktürk/Kemerburgaz, which are lower-density lifestyle markets.
- Much of the regeneration marketed as “Topkapı” is physically in Davutpaşa. The address distinction must be explained honestly.
- Davutpaşa benefits from proximity to genuine Topkapı, the historic peninsula, established employment and transport. This may support future pricing as the regeneration area matures, but growth is not automatic.

### Western residential and employment corridors

- Basın Ekspres, Güneşli and Bağcılar form a mixed office, hotel and residential corridor with meaningful supply.
- Küçükçekmece should not be reduced to Basın Ekspres.
- Halkalı and Atakent are major modern family markets with schools, hospitals, retail, compounds and Marmaray access.
- Başakşehir and Bahçeşehir appeal to family buyers seeking newer housing and organised compounds, but resale timing, supply and commuting must be considered.
- Esenyurt and Beylikdüzü require high selectivity because of large competing supply, price-sensitive local demand and historically heavy international marketing.

### Coastal districts

- Zeytinburnu waterfront offers modern compounds and sea views but often has significant competing inventory and service charges.
- Bakırköy and Ataköy have mature domestic demand and strong transport.
- Yeşilköy and Florya are lower-rise, scarcity-led family and lifestyle markets where micro-location materially affects value.
- **Zeytinburnu comparables:** Büyük Yalı, Yedi Mavi and Pruva are premium compounds and should not be treated as indicative pricing for cheaper regeneration stock nearby. Their pricing does not automatically make a lower-priced neighbouring project attractive. **Source: Confirmed by Mustafa, 2026-09-08.**

### Asian-side investment distinctions

- Kadıköy is among the safest Istanbul districts for domestic demand and resale liquidity, but suitable CBI stock is difficult to find and prime neighbourhoods such as Suadiye, Erenköy, Caddebostan and Fenerbahçe command premium pricing.
- Fikirtepe is Istanbul's largest regeneration project and is heavily marketed to CBI buyers. Large-scale simultaneous delivery creates substantial competition for rent and resale, including around a three-year citizenship holding period.
- Genuine Ataşehir has very limited CBI stock. Batı Ataşehir is a well-planned, middle-class residential area supported by metro, malls, private hospitals, private schools and strong Turkish demand.
- Projects marketed internationally as Ataşehir may actually be in Ümraniye. State the true district and micro-location.
- Ümraniye is highly uneven. The financial-district area can be strong but expensive; newer locations such as Çakmak and Tatlısu require particular caution where CBI-suitable stock is heavily advertised, overpriced or dependent on uncertain future demand.
- **Source for this subsection: Confirmed by Mustafa, 2026-09-08.**

## Established Pera service information

### International partner offices

- Pera can work behind or alongside the introducing office, according to the relationship the office wants to maintain with its client.
- Pera handles the Istanbul side: buyer qualification, market and area advice, property sourcing, developer and resale comparison, viewings, negotiation, purchase coordination, due diligence coordination, completion and after-sales support.
- The introducing office remains informed throughout, and Pera does not seek to displace it from the client relationship.
- **Standard commercial basis discussed with the Japanese HNW office on 2026-09-10:** the net commission actually received by Pera is divided **50/50**. Developer/vendor commission on brand-new property is usually **10% of the purchase price** and is sometimes **8%**. Pera does not charge the buyer a separate agency fee on a brand-new property.
- If an introducing office requires more than its 50% share of the normal net commission, the additional amount would need to be built into the purchase price. Explain this transparently and agree it before presenting the property.
- Before an introduction proceeds, confirm in writing what “net commission” means, what deductions may be made before the split, when commission becomes earned and payable, the treatment of cancellations/refunds, currency and transfer costs, and whether repeat or referred business is protected.

### Purchase and POA support

- Pera can coordinate the property search, negotiation, reservation, independent legal/title checks, valuation, tax-number process, bank introduction, DAB/payment documentation where required, title transfer, DASK, handover and snagging.
- A reservation deposit is usually **USD 5,000–10,000**, subject to the property and seller.
- Under a properly drafted POA, Pera can coordinate or complete authorised administrative actions such as title-deed procedures, utilities, DASK, property-tax and municipal registrations. Bank-account opening and banking transactions remain subject to the individual bank's current KYC rules and acceptance of the POA; never promise that an account can be opened remotely.
- The POA used for a transaction may authorise Pera and the appointed lawyer to handle purchase, renting, utility registration and citizenship legal work where applicable. The actual wording and granted powers must be checked for the individual mandate.
- Transaction taxes, title-deed charges, valuation, translation, notary, apostille/legalisation, DASK, legal fees, banking costs and VAT where applicable must be priced for the specific property and buyer. Use indicative figures only and verify them before issue.

### Owner-use management

- Services may include key holding, periodic inspections, aidat and household-bill administration, DASK/property-tax reminders or payment administration, maintenance coordination with prior approval, cleaning and preparation before arrival, and support when the owner is in Istanbul.

### Property sales mandates

- Standard seller commission: **2% plus VAT**.
- Standard exclusivity period: **six months**, with an unconditional **break clause after three months**. The seller may terminate in writing for any reason and without a termination fee or penalty.
- Pera does **not accept shared/non-exclusive sale listings**.
- If Pera introduces or shows the property to a buyer during the mandate, Pera retains its right to the agreed fee if that buyer or a connected party purchases within the protected period stated in the agreement. The current template uses **six months after termination**.
- If the property is sold during the exclusive term directly by the owner, through another intermediary or by another route, the signed mandate preserves Pera's right to the agreed fee.
- The break-clause notice procedure, introduced-buyer protection and exclusive-period commission protection must be stated in the signed mandate.
- For an initial estimate in a known development, the development, block, floor, layout and gross size may be sufficient. Do not delay a useful working valuation by requesting non-essential details.
- An overseas owner must provide a suitable sale Power of Attorney either to Pera/the appointed lawyer or to the owner's own Turkish lawyer so that local selling and title-transfer procedures can be completed.

#### Overseas-owner resale procedure

- At the initial instruction stage, normally request the **Tapu in PDF format** and any available property photographs. The sale POA is required later if the owner proceeds.
- Do not instruct a lawyer prematurely where an operational request can resolve the issue first. Escalate proportionately.
- Where an existing rental intermediary holds the tenancy documents, first ask the owner to continue requesting them directly by telephone, WhatsApp and email. With the owner's authority, Pera may also request them from the intermediary.
- Do not approach the tenant at the opening stage unless there is a clear operational or legal reason. Premature tenant contact may complicate the sale or the owner's relationship with the tenant.
- If an intermediary refuses to provide the owner's tenancy documents, advise the owner to make a clear written demand and, if non-cooperation continues, warn that legal action may follow. Do not threaten or initiate proceedings on the owner's behalf without authority.
- A foreign-based owner may generally execute a sale POA through an appropriate Turkish consulate or through a local foreign notary followed by the required apostille/legalisation process. The correct route, form and translation requirements must be confirmed for the owner's nationality, residence and intended use.
- A sale POA can be restricted to the identified property and necessary sale procedures. Typical requested powers may include obtaining and submitting property documents, Land Registry portal applications, signing sale documents, completing title transfer, paying authorised taxes and administrative charges, communicating with building management and municipalities, closing or transferring relevant utilities, and directing the sale proceeds to the owner's nominated Turkish account.
- Pera can ordinarily coordinate the sale appointment, completion documentation, relevant payments and taxes, title transfer and a secure route for sale proceeds. A client may appoint an independent lawyer separately at additional cost.
- **Legal partner currently identified:** SEA Law, used for specialist matters involving international clients. Confirm the appropriate lawyer, scope and fee before making a client introduction.
- Check whether annual municipal property tax is up to date and obtain the municipality document required for the Land Registry sale process. The commonly stated title-transfer tax allocation is 2% to the buyer and 2% to the seller, but verify current law and the transaction's contractual allocation before advising.
- For property previously used for Turkish citizenship, ask whether the Tapu was updated to the owner's Turkish identity and whether the three-year restriction was removed after expiry.
- Banking, tax, consular, apostille, POA and citizenship-title procedures are **time-sensitive**. Verify the current rule before presenting them as legal requirements. In particular, recheck the payment-currency and bank-account requirements applicable to Turkish citizens before advising on receipt of sale proceeds.

### Rental services

- Let-only service: **8% + VAT**.
- Let-and-manage service: **12% + VAT**.
- Services may include tenant selection and approval, bilingual tenancy contracts (including Turkish-Japanese for Japanese clients), a two-month deposit, utility transfers, DASK/property-tax administration and reporting on request.
- No repair, maintenance or third-party cost should be authorised without the owner's prior written approval.

Record any client-specific variation separately. Do not assume these terms apply to another service or mandate.
